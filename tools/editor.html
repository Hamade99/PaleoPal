The proportions as handles on the animal. Hover a square to name it, click to select, then drag — a selected handle is the only one a drag moves, so two landmarks close together stop fighting over the pointer. Each handle moves one thing. The animal is pinned by its feet, so it will not wander while you work on it. These write the same numbers the Species tab puts on sliders: another way in, not another set of values.

One row per growth stage, shared by every species. These are what make growing up a change in shape rather than a change in size.

A coat swaps the three body ramps and paints a pattern onto the mark layer. Patterns are drawn from the animal's own spine, so they follow whatever shape the proportions give it.

Drag the hat onto the animal. The species decides roughly where on the skull headgear belongs; this is where one particular hat wants to sit on one particular animal. On all stages the fit is in sprite units, so it scales with the animal and one placement covers the whole growth series. Pick a stage instead and you are overriding just that age — a crown in proportion on an adult skull is a bucket on a hatchling. A stage that has its own say is marked with a star, here and in the strip underneath.
